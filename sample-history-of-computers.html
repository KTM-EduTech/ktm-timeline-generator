The History of Computers

From mechanical calculators to everyday AI

  1. Event 1 of 10.

    1822Mechanical era

    Difference Engine proposed

    Charles Babbage designs a mechanical calculator to compute and print mathematical tables without human error.

  2. Event 2 of 10.

    1843Mechanical eraKey milestone

    Ada Lovelace publishes her Notes

    Lovelace's notes on the Analytical Engine include a method for calculating Bernoulli numbers, widely regarded as the first published computer program.

  3. Event 3 of 10.

    1936Electronic era

    Turing describes the universal machine

    Alan Turing's paper "On Computable Numbers" sets out an abstract machine that defines what can be computed.

  4. Event 4 of 10.

    1946Electronic eraKey milestone

    ENIAC unveiled

    One of the first general-purpose electronic computers is presented to the public at the University of Pennsylvania. It filled a large room and used about 18,000 vacuum tubes.

  5. Event 5 of 10.

    1947Electronic era

    The transistor

    Researchers at Bell Labs demonstrate the transistor, which later replaces bulky vacuum tubes and makes smaller computers possible.

  6. Event 6 of 10.

    1971Electronic era

    First commercial microprocessor

    Intel releases the 4004, placing a complete central processing unit on a single chip.

  7. Event 7 of 10.

    1981Personal & connected

    The IBM Personal Computer

    The IBM PC brings computers into offices and homes and sets a standard many manufacturers follow.

  8. Event 8 of 10.

    1989 – 1991Personal & connectedKey milestone

    The World Wide Web

    Tim Berners-Lee proposes the Web in 1989 at CERN; the first website goes online in 1991.

  9. Event 9 of 10.

    2007Personal & connected

    Touchscreen smartphones go mainstream

    Pocket-sized computers with touchscreens, apps and mobile internet become part of daily life.

  10. Event 10 of 10.

    Early 2020sPersonal & connected

    AI assistants for everyone

    Large language models become widely available tools for writing, coding and learning.
